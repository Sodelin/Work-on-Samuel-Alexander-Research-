# How far the specieslike bridge extends

**Status.** The graph arguments below are written deductions from the [classification manuscript's binary graph](https://github.com/avg-netizen/biological-unavoidability/blob/main/paper.md#2-an-explicit-binary-avoiding-population), [Alexander's 2013 inspecies theory](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.2869), and [his 2026 specieslike-cluster definitions](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1). They are not a claim about real species or literature priority. The [species bridge](../lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/SpeciesBridge.lean) and [exact two-cone classification](../lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/SpeciesCones.lean) are Lean checked for `P_s`; the generic IAP equivalence, root-cone criterion, strict-layering lemma, and counterexamples in **this note** are written arguments only. See the [formalization scope](../FORMALIZATION.md).

**Prior-work warning.** Alexander's own [2013 Proposition 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.2869#S5) proves cofinite descendants for members of any *inspecies*. His [2026 Example 14(1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S6) already exhibits the multiple-root, overlapping-cone pattern. The point here is to connect those earlier ideas to the later *word-avoiding graph*, not to claim either general pattern as new.

## The general reason the proof works

Call a vertex `w` a **strict descendant** of `v` when a nonempty directed path goes from `v` to `w`, as in Alexander's paper. Consider a graph on `ℕ` with this property:

> **Cofinal reachability:** for every vertex `v`, some bound `N_v` exists such that `v` is an ancestor of every `w ≥ N_v`.

Then the set of non-descendants of `v` is contained in the finite prefix `{0,…,N_v−1}`. Therefore **every subset `S` of the vertices satisfies the identical ancestor point axiom**: within `S`, every `v` has only finitely many non-descendants. If `S` is infinite, it also has Alexander's **reflection property**: deleting finitely many non-descendants from `S` leaves infinitely many descendants of each `v∈S`. Any two vertices have a common sufficiently late descendant, so the whole graph is weakly connected. The whole vertex set is convex automatically. Thus, if the graph also satisfies the background infinite-biosphere conditions, its whole vertex set is a specieslike cluster.

This is a *sufficient* structural criterion, not the definition of a specieslike cluster. The exact whole-graph criterion from Definitions 2–4 is: the graph is weakly connected and, for each vertex, its descendant set is **finite or cofinite**. Whole-set convexity is automatic. As a counterexample to the converse of the stronger criterion, take the edges `0→1`, `0→2`, `2→3→4→…` with birthdate equal to vertex number. The whole graph is connected and specieslike, yet leaf `1` has no descendants and certainly does not reach a cofinal tail.

There is a useful **exact** version of the generalization. Write `D(v)` for the strict descendants of `v` in an ambient graph `G`. The following three statements are equivalent:

1. Every vertex has `D(v)` finite or `G \ D(v)` finite (the whole graph has IAP).
2. Every subset of `G` has IAP.
3. Every infinite subset of `G` has the reflection property relative to `G`.

The first statement implies the second by intersecting either finite side with a subset. It implies the third because a vertex with finitely many ambient descendants makes reflection vacuous, while an infinite subset meets any cofinite descendant set infinitely. Conversely, if both `D(v)` and its complement are infinite for some `v`, take `v` together with infinitely many vertices from **each** side: this subset fails IAP. Taking `v` together with infinitely many non-descendants and no descendants gives an infinite subset that fails reflection. This equivalence uses only Alexander's [IAP and reflection definitions](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1); it does not require the special `+1,+2` edges. It is a direct deduction from those definitions, not a claimed new literature result. Connectedness and the background biosphere axioms remain separate requirements for calling the whole graph specieslike. In the quotient Boolean algebra of vertex subsets modulo finite sets, this says that every principal descendant set represents either `0` or `1`; this is only an order-theoretic restatement. For **any infinite digraph**, the whole vertex set is an inspecies (a minimal infinite ancestrally closed set) if and only if every vertex has **cofinite** strict descendants. If `V \ D(v)` were infinite, `V \ (D(v) union {v})` would be a proper infinite ancestrally closed subset, contradicting minimality. Including `{v}` in the removed set handles a terminal vertex with no descendants. Conversely, any infinite ancestrally closed set meets every cofinite descendant set, so ancestral closure forces it to contain every vertex. This is an elementary specialization of the established [2013 Proposition 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.2869#S5), with the reverse direction spelled out. Therefore, when **every vertex has infinitely many descendants**, whole-graph IAP is equivalent to the whole graph being an inspecies. The infinite-descendants condition matters: a connected chain with a terminal leaf has whole-graph IAP but is not an inspecies.

## What this says for the exact binary avoiding graph

For every target word `s`, the unlabelled graph `P_s` has vertices `ℕ`, with `u→w` exactly when `w≥2` and `w=u+1` or `w=u+2`. The labels vary with `s`; this geometry does not. Its strict descendant relation has the exact form

```text
v is an ancestor of w  ⇔  v < w and w ≥ 2.
```

For `v≥1`, the consecutive edges reach every later vertex. Root `0` reaches `2` and then every later vertex, while it cannot reach `1`. The exact formula proves cofinal reachability, hence **IAP for every subset**. It does *not* say every subset is specieslike. For example, `{0,1}` is disconnected. A subset can also be connected but fail convexity: `{0,2,4}` contains edges `0→2→4`, but omits `3`, which lies between `2` and `4` along `2→3→4`.

There is an older route to the same cofinite-descendant conclusion. Alexander's [2013 Definition 4](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.2869#S4) calls a minimal infinite ancestrally closed set an **inspecies**, and his [Proposition 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.2869#S5) says each of its members is an ancestor of all but finitely many members. The whole `P_s` is an inspecies: it is infinite and ancestrally closed; any infinite ancestrally closed subset has unbounded vertex indices, and every sufficiently large vertex has *every* earlier vertex as an ancestor, so that subset must equal the whole graph. The exact descendant formula above proves this specialization directly and makes the finite exceptional set explicit. Applying Proposition 6 and then the 2026 IAP definition also gives the whole-graph bridge. Subset IAP follows separately by restriction: intersecting a finite exceptional set with a subset remains finite.

This yields a stronger conditional word-classification corollary: even when eligible **edge-labelled binary populations** are restricted to those whose underlying whole graph is an inspecies, the unavoidable words are exactly the eventually periodic words. Alexander's positive theorem remains true after restriction, and for a non-eventually-periodic target the classification manuscript's `P_s` is an avoiding witness in this smaller class. This is a direct cross-paper consequence, not a new proof of either input theorem; the classification's positive half is not Lean formalized here.

For this particular graph, reflection has a simple characterization: a subset satisfies it **if and only if it is empty or infinite**. Every vertex has infinitely many descendants in the ambient graph. A nonempty finite subset therefore fails reflection at each of its members. An infinite subset has infinitely many members above any finite bound, and all sufficiently late members descend from any specified vertex.

## Exactly two maximal clusters when common ancestry is added

The whole `P_s` is the unique maximal specieslike subset, simply because it is specieslike and contains every subset. It fails Alexander's **common ancestor property**: the two roots `0` and `1` have no ancestor in the graph. Adding the common-ancestor and reflection conditions changes the maximal subsets. Define

```text
C₀ = {0,2,3,4,…}   and   C₁ = {1,2,3,4,…}.
```

**Claim.** These are exactly the inclusion-maximal subsets of `P_s` satisfying IAP, convexity, common ancestry, and reflection. They are also connected, hence specieslike. They overlap in the infinite tail `{2,3,4,…}` and differ only in which root they contain.

**Proof.** `0` is a common ancestor of `C₀`, and `1` of `C₁`; every other member lies later along consecutive edges. Each cone is connected and infinite, so it has IAP and reflection by the results above. `C₀` omits only vertex `1`, which has no ancestor, and `C₁` omits only vertex `0`, likewise with no ancestor. Hence both are convex in the *ambient* graph. Neither can be enlarged except by adding the other root; that produces the whole graph, which has no common ancestor. Conversely, any set with common ancestor `v=0` is contained in `C₀`, while one with common ancestor `v≥1` is contained in `C₁`. A proper subset of the corresponding cone is therefore not maximal in this four-property class. QED.

This exact two-cone classification is a stronger, graph-specific consequence than our original whole-graph observation, **but the cluster pattern itself is already in Alexander's paper**. His [Example 14(1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S6) constructs a different `k`-root biosphere with exactly `k` maximal four-property cones; its `k=2` case also has two cones overlapping in an infinite tail. Our statement specializes that pattern to the independently defined avoiding graph `P_s`. It illustrates why [Theorem 13](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S6) guarantees existence without unique boundaries, and it does not claim that `C₀` or `C₁` is an empirical species.

The [Lean cone module](../lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/SpeciesCones.lean) proves the exact `P_s` statement, including that these are the **only** maximal sets satisfying the four properties. Its conclusion is about this graph, while the root-cone criterion below remains a written generalization.

The comparison has a sharp **labelled** distinction. In Example 14(1) with `k=2`, every nonroot is in a generation `G_{n+1}` and has both parents in the immediately preceding two-vertex generation `G_n`. Give its two incoming edges the two binary labels, in either order. For any binary word `s` and any length `m`, start at a vertex of `G_m` and choose parents backwards with labels `s(m−1),…,s(0)`. This gives a path of length `m` starting in the finite root generation. The tree of such root-started matching paths is finitely branching and has every finite height, so König's lemma gives an infinite path spelling `s`. Thus **every valid binary labelling of that earlier two-cone graph realizes every binary word**. The `P_s` graph's `+2` skip edges prevent this strict generation-by-generation argument; its two-cone geometry can coexist with avoidance. This is a direct comparison of the two cited constructions, not a claim of a new general theorem or literature priority.

The same backward argument applies to any strictly layered population with a finite initial root layer, finite outdegree, nonempty generations of arbitrarily large index, edges only from one generation to the next, and **every vertex outside the initial layer** receiving an incoming edge of every alphabet label from the preceding layer. It makes **every** infinite word realizable. Requiring coverage only at nonroots would leave open the possibility of new roots in later layers and would be insufficient. Dropping strict layering invalidates this argument, as `P_s` demonstrates; it does not say that every graph with skip edges can avoid a word. This elementary lemma describes exactly which structural assumption separates the two examples.

## A root-cone criterion, and why its hypothesis matters

The cone calculation extends beyond the particular offsets `+1,+2`. In a birthdate-ordered digraph where **each vertex is a root or a strict descendant of some root**, let `K_r` contain a root `r` and all its descendants. Suppose **each root cone satisfies IAP**. Then the inclusion-maximal sets satisfying IAP, convexity, common ancestry, and reflection are exactly the root cones. Conversely, if that maximal family equals the family of all root cones, every root cone must have IAP. Thus, under the stated root-coverage hypothesis, the all-root-cones conclusion holds **if and only if** each root cone has IAP. A cone is connected by paths from its root and has that root as common ancestor. It is convex in the ambient graph and contains all descendants of any of its members, so it reflects infinite descendant sets. By hypothesis it also has IAP. Any set with common ancestor `v` lies inside `K_r` for a root `r` above `v`; distinct root cones cannot contain one another, since a root has no ancestor. Therefore the qualifying root cones are precisely the maximal sets.

This is a direct deduction from Alexander's definitions and his related observation before [Example 15](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S6) that if IAP is dropped, the maximal sets are root clades. It is **not** a newly identified general cluster theorem. The extra IAP hypothesis is necessary for this simple answer. For example, take one root `a₀` and vertices `a_n,b_n` with edges `a_n→a_{n+1}`, `b_n→b_{n+1}`, and `a_n→b_n`. The one root reaches the whole graph, but `b₀` has infinitely many `b` descendants and infinitely many `a` non-descendants, so its root cone fails IAP. Even a shared eventual descendant or one undirected end is too weak a substitute for the finite-or-cofinite condition. Adding an isolated root to `P_s` leaves its old infinite component's ancestry intact but adds a third maximal four-property cluster, the singleton new root; the exact count of two depends on the actual roots.

The IAP failure above persists even **within the binary population axioms**. Take two rails `a₀,a₁,…` and `b₀,b₁,…`. For each rail vertex at index `n≥2`, add edges from the same rail at indices `n−1` and `n−2` and give them complementary binary labels. Add rungs `a_n→b_n` for `n≥2`, with either label, and use birthdates `t(a_n)=2n`, `t(b_n)=2n+1`. There are four roots, finite outdegree, finite birthdate prefixes, and every nonroot receives both labels. Any two vertices have a common later descendant on the `b` rail, and the undirected graph has one end, but `b_n` has infinitely many `b` descendants and infinitely many `a` non-descendants. Hence the whole graph fails IAP. This labelled counterexample shows that common future, one end, and the population axioms together still do not force the cofinite-descendant property.

## The label boundary remains real

The induced labelled graph on either cone need not be an eligible binary population: vertex `2` retains only one of its two differently labelled parents, because the other root was removed. So the binary unavoidable-sequence theorem cannot simply be rerun *inside* either maximal cone. The whole-graph specieslike deduction and the two-cone classification are statements about **unlabelled ancestry**. Any further theorem about parent-label words in maximal clusters must specify how boundary labels are retained or repaired, and check the population axioms again. This is the next precise interface question.
