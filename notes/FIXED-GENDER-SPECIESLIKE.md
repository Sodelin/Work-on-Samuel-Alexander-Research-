A stronger productive cap-three inspecies core and a [retained-subtype classification](../lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/FixedGenderReindex.lean) are also checked. It uses the exact binary construction and gender lift in [the classification manuscript, §§2–3](https://github.com/avg-netizen/biological-unavoidability/blob/main/paper.md) and the specieslike axioms in [Alexander (2026), Definitions 2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1). No claim of literature priority or of an empirical species model is made.

## Why the direct lift needs a small repair

Let $`s : \mathbb{N} \to \{0,1\}`$ be a binary target, and let $`r(2j)=s(j)`$, $`r(2j+1)=1-s(j)`$. In the base edge-labelled graph $`P_{s}`$, the edge $`0\to 2`$ has label $`1-s(1)`$, while both $`1\to 2`$ and $`1\to 3`$ have label $`s(1)`$. The fixed-vertex-gender lift replaces each base vertex $`v`$ by $`(v,0)`$ and $`(v,1)`$. A base edge $`u\to w`$ labelled $`a`$ becomes $`(u,a)\to (w,b)`$ for **each** $`b\in \{0,1\}`$.

Consequently $`(0,s(1))`$ and $`(1,1-s(1))`$ have neither parents nor children: they are isolated root copies. Every other root copy has a child, and every copy over a base vertex $`w\ge 2`$ has two incoming parents. The full lift therefore has three weak components, so its entire vertex set is **not** a specieslike cluster under Alexander's connectedness condition.

Delete just those two isolated vertices, retaining the induced graph $`Q_{s}`$ on all other copies. This removes no edge at all. The remaining two roots are $`(0,1-s(1))`$ and $`(1,s(1))`$; every $`(w,b)`$ for $`w\ge 2`$ still has one parent of each gender, inherited from the two complementary base edges. Each vertex has at most four children, birthdates $`t(v,b)=v`$ have finite initial segments, and there are infinitely many vertices. Thus $`Q_{s}`$ remains an eligible fixed-vertex-gendered binary population. As an induced subgraph of the lift, it cannot acquire a path spelling $`s`$. The classification manuscript proves that the lift avoids every non-eventually-periodic $`s`$, so $`Q_{s}`$ avoids it as well.

## Why the remaining graph is specieslike

The two copies at base index $`2`$ share the retained root parents. For each $`w\ge 3`$, both copies at index $`w`$ share an incoming parent copy at $`w-1`$, already connected to that core. By induction all nonroot copies and both retained roots lie in one weak component.

Take a retained vertex $`(v,a)`$. If it has no child, it has no descendants. If it has a child at base index $`u\ge 2`$, then it reaches **both** copies at $`u`$. For the base edge $`u\to u+1`$, choose the reachable copy at $`u`$ whose gender equals that edge's label; this lifted edge reaches both copies at $`u+1`$. Repeat at each later index, choosing the source gender needed for the next consecutive base edge. Therefore $`(v,a)`$ is an ancestor of both copies at every index $`w\ge u`$. It has only finitely many non-descendants in $`Q_{s}`$. Each vertex thus satisfies one side of Alexander's identical ancestor point axiom: finitely many descendants or finitely many non-descendants.

The **whole** vertex set of $`Q_{s}`$ is genealogically convex automatically. It is also convex as a subset of the original full lift, because the two deleted vertices are isolated and cannot lie between retained vertices on a directed path. Hence the connected $`Q_{s}`$ is a specieslike cluster. In fact it is the unique infinite weak component of the original lift, and adding an isolated root would break connectedness; it is inclusion-maximal as a specieslike subset of that fixed ambient lift. It also satisfies reflection: a leaf has no ambient descendants, while every nonleaf has infinitely many descendants inside $`Q_{s}`$.

**An inspecies distinction.** This repaired graph need not be an inspecies, despite being connected and whole-specieslike. For every odd base index $`u=2j+1`$ with $`u\ge 3`$, its two outgoing base edges have the same label $`s(j+1)`$. The opposite-gender copy $`(u,1-s(j+1))`$ is therefore a terminal leaf of $`Q_{s}`$. There are infinitely many such leaves, and deleting any one of them leaves a proper infinite ancestrally closed subset. This distinguishes $`Q_{s}`$ from the unlifted $`P_{s}`$, whose whole graph is an inspecies.

**Binary classification consequence.** If one restricts eligible fixed-vertex-gendered binary populations further by requiring their entire graph to be connected and specieslike, the unavoidable words are still exactly the eventually periodic ones. The positive half is Alexander's result for all eligible populations. For each non-eventually-periodic word, the cleaned $`Q_{s}`$ is a counterexample in the restricted class. The graph obligations are now checked. The new positive binary theorem and cap-three retained-subtype iff are encoded; the cap-four connected/specieslike formulation here is the corresponding mathematical corollary.

The two roots of $`Q_{s}`$ still have no common ancestor. In fact **no entire eligible binary population can have Alexander's common-ancestor property**, under the simple-edge definition used here. Choose an earliest nonroot by the finite-birthdate-prefix axiom. Its differently labelled incoming edges must come from two distinct, earlier vertices, and the earliest-nonroot choice makes both of those vertices roots. No vertex can be an ancestor of two different roots. The same argument gives at least $`k`$ roots for $`k`$ required labels. The [binary natural-date Lean specialization](../lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/RootObstruction.lean) checks the two-root obstruction, but the general $`k`$-label, real-date version is written only. Common ancestry is therefore a meaningful added condition on **proper subsets**, whose induced graphs may lose incoming-label coverage; it is not a viable whole-population restriction. The [generalization note for the unlifted graph](SPECIESLIKE-GENERALIZATION.md) analyzes those subsets.
