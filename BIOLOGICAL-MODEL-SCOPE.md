# Which biological mechanisms the graph represents

**Scope of this note.** The [2013 sequence model](https://arxiv.org/html/1212.0186v2) and [2026 specieslike model](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1) use related parenthood graphs but impose different axioms. Our Lean certificates concern those mathematical predicates under their stated restrictions. The comparisons below identify modeling choices before interpreting a theorem as a claim about organisms, cells, or species. The source statements and our proposed extensions are separated explicitly.

## Two graph classes that should stay separate

| Model | What the cited source requires | Consequence for this notebook |
|---|---|---|
| [Alexander 2013, Definition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/1212.0186v2) | A directed **simple** graph with a single gender/role label on each parent-to-child edge; finitely many roots; finite children and finite birthdate sublevels; every nonroot has an incoming edge of **every** required label. Fixed gender of a vertex is introduced as a special case, with its outgoing edges inheriting that vertex's label. | The unavoidable-word theorem and the [multiple-root obstruction](lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/RootObstruction.lean) depend on this strong incoming-label coverage. An organism may play different edge roles for different children in the general model. The [fixed-vertex-gender repair](notes/FIXED-GENDER-SPECIESLIKE.md) is an extra restriction, not a broader biological model. |
| [Alexander 2026, Definition 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1) | A birthdated, infinite parenthood graph with finite children and finite earlier-time sets. It does **not** require each nonroot to have two parents or one parent of every label. Its [specieslike Definition 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S3) separately asks for connectedness, IAP, and convexity. | One-parent clonal reproduction is allowed. The positive binary unavoidability theorem does not transfer to this larger class: an infinite chain whose edges all carry label $`A`$ avoids the periodic word $`BBBB\ldots`$. Whole-graph common-ancestor failure is likewise conditional on the stronger labelled model; a single clonal chain has a common ancestor. |

The single edge $`u\to v`$ in the 2013 simple graph carries only one label. **Inference:** if one parent contributes *both* required roles to the same offspring, the ordered pair $`(u,v)`$ would need two labelled incidences, a reproduction-event node, or another representation. The same individual taking different roles for **different** offspring is already permitted by edge labels. Changing the representation changes the path language and population axioms, so the sequence theorems would need to be stated and checked again.

## What Alexander has already discussed

The [2026 specieslike paper, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S3) explicitly considers a variation with one or two parents, occasional sexual reproduction, and horizontal gene transfer (HGT) as a possible parent-child relation. It also warns that its ancestor point concerns **genealogical**, not genetic, ancestry. [Example 14(2)–(5)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S6) already covers two-way, one-way, and nested permanent splits and repeated contributions from outer populations. We should not present those mechanisms as new additions to his model. The [2013 inspecies paper, §6](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.2869) also discusses internodons and benchmark biospheres, and its §2.2 explicitly notes that graph vertices could be chosen at an organism or chromosome scale.

Two elementary examples show why model scope matters:

- An infinite **clonal chain** with one parent per new vertex satisfies the 2026 background graph axioms and whole-graph IAP: each vertex reaches every sufficiently late vertex. It does not meet the two-label incoming-parent axiom from 2013.
- An infinite **clonal binary tree** satisfies the 2026 background graph axioms but its whole graph fails IAP: a vertex with an infinite descendant branch also has infinitely many non-descendants in a sibling branch. A specieslike cluster could be a proper subset; the whole tree is not one under Definition 4. These are deductions from the definitions, not empirical conclusions about clonal organisms.

## Genetic inheritance is another graph layer

[Dress, Moulton, Steel, and Wu (2010), Section 2.1](https://www.math.canterbury.ac.nz/~m.steel/Non_UC/files/research/species_jtb.pdf) already distinguishes organismal genealogy from surviving genetic ancestry. Their broad parent relation also allows lateral gene transfer, and they define a genetic subgraph by retaining arcs that transmit material surviving in an observed set. Their main cluster theorem concerns a **finite** directed acyclic graph and clusters of an observed set $`X`$, so it does not supply Alexander's infinite IAP theorem. A gene-aware extension here would refine an established modeling distinction, not introduce it for the first time.

[Wong et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/iyae100) define ancestral recombination graphs whose nodes can represent haploid genomes and whose edges carry **genomic intervals** inherited from ancestors. Those edges can span generations and need not be immediate organism-birth edges. The authors distinguish the genome graph from the individual pedigree. This is a closer starting point for a gene-aware extension than simply relabelling Alexander's organism edges. [Shapiro et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1218198) report habitat-associated divergence and recombination patterns in *Vibrio cyclitrophicus* populations; this is empirical context for why gene exchange may matter, not a proof of a specieslike graph axiom.

**Proposed extension, not a result here:** keep organism **birth-parent** edges, time-stamped transfer events, and locus-specific inheritance as distinct relations. A post-birth donor-to-recipient organism edge may violate Alexander's $`t(\text{parent})<t(\text{child})`$ condition when the donor was born later; even when the donor is older, treating transfer as birth-parenthood changes reachability and potentially IAP. One would have to specify the vertex/event types, time order, path labels, and the intended ancestor relation, then reprove every claim that uses them. Choosing such a model by reproduction and inheritance mechanism is more informative than dividing organisms only by broad taxonomic group.

This notebook's specieslike statements therefore say that the **specified graph** satisfies specified formal predicates. They do not establish a dataset's parent edges, a physiological or genetic mechanism, or empirical species boundaries.
