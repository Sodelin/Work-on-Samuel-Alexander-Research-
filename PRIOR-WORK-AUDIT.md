## The main correction to our initial framing

The genealogy observations have stronger precedents in Alexander's **own** work than our first comparison showed. In [*Infinite graphs in systematic biology* (2013), Definition 4 and Proposition 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.2869#S5), an *inspecies* is a minimal infinite ancestrally closed set, and every member of one is ancestor of all but finitely many others. The unlabelled avoiding graph `P_s` in the [2026 classification manuscript](https://github.com/avg-netizen/biological-unavoidability/blob/main/paper.md) is itself its **unique inspecies**: any infinite ancestrally closed subset has unbounded vertex numbers, and every sufficiently late vertex has every earlier vertex as ancestor. Thus its cofinite-descendant property is already a specialization of Proposition 6. The explicit reachability calculation remains valuable as a short direct proof and Lean target.

Likewise, [Alexander's 2026 *Specieslike clusters* paper, Example 14(1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S6), already gives a different graph with exactly `k` maximal root-descendant clusters satisfying IAP, convexity, common ancestry, and reflection. Its `k=2` case anticipates our two-cone pattern. Our exact classification of `P_s`'s two cones is a **specialization to the word-avoiding graph**, not a novel general cluster mechanism. The same paper's [Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S4) says two infinite specieslike clusters with infinitely many shared **generators** have generator sets differing by only finitely many vertices. It does not generally say that the clusters themselves differ by only finitely many vertices.

The remaining interesting interface is that the **same target-dependent graph** serves a word-avoidance construction and these ancestry/cluster roles. The fixed-vertex-gender lift gives a further precise compatibility test after isolated roots are removed. We found no exact prior statement of that linkage in this bounded search; this is a research lead, **not a priority claim**.

There is a concrete reason the cross-paper link is more than a renamed example. Alexander's earlier [Example 14(1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1#S6) has a two-cone case, but its edges connect **consecutive finite generations**. If its two incoming edges at each nonroot are assigned the two binary labels, then backward choices from arbitrarily late generations and König's lemma give a path for *every* infinite binary word. It therefore cannot be an avoiding witness under any valid binary labelling. The classification manuscript's `P_s` has skip edges, and [the exact comparison](notes/SPECIESLIKE-GENERALIZATION.md) proves why the earlier cone example does not itself supply this word-avoidance bridge. This is a direct deduction from the two constructions, with no priority claim.

The [generalization note](notes/SPECIESLIKE-GENERALIZATION.md) makes the boundary exact: for any infinite graph, its whole vertex set is an inspecies exactly when every vertex has cofinite descendants. Whole-graph IAP is weaker because it also permits vertices with **finite** descendants. When every vertex has infinitely many descendants, those two whole-graph conditions coincide. This is an elementary deduction in the setting of Alexander's earlier inspecies theorem, not an independent new species concept. The repaired fixed-gender graph illustrates the boundary: it is connected and whole-specieslike, but has infinitely many terminal copies and is **not** an inspecies.

## Targeted prior art for the sharp Thue-Morse theorem

The [classification manuscript, Section 5](https://github.com/avg-netizen/biological-unavoidability/blob/main/paper.md) explicitly left useful quantitative bounds for its fixed Thue-Morse target open. The present `3*L(v)<=8*v-1` inequality and complete equality set therefore extend that **particular manuscript**. They are not results in Alexander's 2013 paper, which supplied the population problem and model. The [Lean theorem](lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/SharpThueMorse.lean) checks the exact edge relation and target phase. An independent targeted search did not locate an earlier equivalent theorem, but this is not a first-in-literature certificate.

## Counterexamples that keep the generalization honest

1. **One root and shared future do not imply IAP.** The directed ladder in the [generalization note](notes/SPECIESLIKE-GENERALIZATION.md) has one root and common later descendants, yet `b₀` has infinitely many descendants and infinitely many non-descendants.
2. **The exact number of maximal clusters depends on roots.** Add an isolated root to `P_s`: the old ancestry and its unique infinite component remain, but a third maximal four-property cluster appears as the singleton new root.
3. **Taking a maximal cluster can destroy the label axiom.** In either `P_s` cone, vertex `2` loses one of its differently labelled parents. The unavoidable-word theorem cannot be applied to that induced labelled graph without checking a new boundary construction.
4. **The fixed-gender lift is initially disconnected.** Two root copies are isolated. The [pruned construction](notes/FIXED-GENDER-SPECIESLIKE.md) removes exactly those copies; calling the unpruned whole lift specieslike would be false.
5. **Whole-population common ancestry is impossible for two labels under simple edges.** An earliest nonroot needs two incoming labels from two distinct earlier vertices, both roots. No vertex can ancestor both roots. The common-ancestor axiom can therefore be imposed only on a proper subset in this model, where label coverage must be rechecked.
6. **A common future and one graph end do not force IAP, even in an eligible binary population.** The [two-rail labelled construction](notes/SPECIESLIKE-GENERALIZATION.md) has four roots, complete incoming label coverage, common later descendants and one undirected end; every `b`-rail vertex still has infinitely many descendants and non-descendants.
