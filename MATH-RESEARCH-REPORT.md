## The idea in one minute

An ancestry graph has organisms as vertices and labelled parent relationships as edges. A path through the graph spells a word: the sequence of labels encountered along that lineage. Alexander asked which infinite words must appear in every population satisfying particular parent and birth conditions. His positive theorem and the separate classification manuscript are credited as source results. Our project asked sharper follow-up questions about **how long a forbidden word can appear**, **how few children a construction needs**, **what ancestry clusters can be guaranteed**, and **what survives when a complicated history is simplified**.

The work is AI assisted. Some results were translated into Lean 4 definitions and proofs; others have exact rational computations or written arguments. A Lean check supports the theorem that was actually encoded, under its actual hypotheses. It is not a certificate of worldwide novelty, source fidelity, or biological applicability. [Question provenance](QUESTION-LEDGER.md) and the [research architecture](RESEARCH-ARCHITECTURE.md) record those distinctions.

## Ten proposals: the present answers

These are the ten proposals *we formulated for this notebook*, not ten historical conjectures solved on Alexander's behalf. Their original wording is retained in [Ten research ideas](TEN-RESEARCH-IDEAS.md); the newer [solution ledger](TEN-SOLUTIONS.md) records exact statements and proof boundaries. “Answered” below means the proposal's stated main question has a scoped answer; follow-on refinements remain open.

| Proposal | What was established | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Smallest child cap with permanent vertex labels | A directed-line-graph construction avoids any prescribed non-eventually-periodic binary target with at most two children per vertex, while the whole retained population has the stated inspecies and reflection properties. A cap below two is impossible. [Proof and model](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/a2644182b147eb40816abb278e00c3c6d4709eb8/notes/CAP-TWO-SPECIES.md). | The construction has three roots; optimal root count and literature priority are separate. |
| 2. Minimum crossing width | At the critical child cap, an eventual minimum number of edges crossing each birth-order cut forces a rigid tail: each vertex connects to the next `k` vertices. [General theorem](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/a2644182b147eb40816abb278e00c3c6d4709eb8/notes/GENERAL-RIGIDITY-THEOREM.md). | One isolated minimum cut does not imply a rigid tail; larger widths remain to classify. |
| 3. Finite ports | A critical population tail can be represented with a fixed finite collection of crossing-edge ports and a complete update schedule. An eventually periodic *complete schedule* entails every infinite label word is realized. [Encoding](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/notes/PORT-ENCODING.md). | Finite ports alone do not make an arbitrary schedule periodic. |
| 4. Every Thue–Morse matching height | The maximum length from every starting vertex has a closed form, and a ten-coordinate binary recurrence computes it. The proof concerns actual graph paths and actual attained maxima, including start zero. [Full-height proof](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/a2644182b147eb40816abb278e00c3c6d4709eb8/notes/FULL-HEIGHT-PROOF.md). | A literal integer-module 2-regularity wrapper remains a separate uncompiled refinement; minimality of the ten coordinates is not claimed. |
| 5. Sequence phase | Rebuilding both the graph and target after a Thue–Morse shift preserves the optimal leading coefficient `8/3`. Equality starts are classified, and a finite frontier procedure computes every phase/start maximum. [Phase algorithm](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/notes/PHASE-HEIGHT-FORMALIZATION.md). | A joint phase/index digit formula and complexity bound remain open. |
| 6. Quantitative avoidance | An explicit period or antiperiod break modulus supplies a bound on matching length. Conversely, mere aperiodicity allows finite matching maxima that grow faster than any proposed function along selected starts. [Modulus theorem](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/notes/QUANTITATIVE-MODULUS-THEOREM.md). | An effective clock needs an effective supplied modulus. |
| 7. Finite target edits | Changing an initial segment preserves the optimal `8/3` coefficient. The sharp universal additive allowance is `8m−1`; a Lean-checked finite algorithm finds each edited target's best integer allowance. [Refinement](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/notes/REFINEMENT-INDIVIDUAL-FINITE-EDIT.md). | The individual optimum modules are included in the 405-endpoint core audit at the cited proof commit. No efficient runtime claim is made. |
| 8. Productive core | Removing vertices with only finitely many descendants can preserve the relevant eligibility, child cap, and path language under the theorem's assumptions, yielding an inspecies under whole-graph IAP. Counterexamples separate several notions of cluster maximality. [Proof](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/notes/PRODUCTIVE-CORE-THEOREM.md). | The general population, cluster, and CA/REF variants have different hypotheses. |
| 9. Boundary repair | For a fixed infinite retained vertex set, finitely many missing incoming-parent labels are exactly the condition for a finite deletion-only repair. The canonical repair retains the most edges and creates the fewest roots under that policy. [Proof](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/notes/BOUNDARY-REPAIR-THEOREM.md). | Repair can break connectedness and need not preserve every specieslike property. |
| 10. Stateful cellular automaton | For a specified synthetic three-state rule, the best fixed two-label static eastward certificate has support one, yet a state potential rules out every nonzero horizontal finite-support spaceship motion. [Real-hull result](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/notes/STATEFUL-CA-REAL.md). | This is a precise comparison for one synthetic rule, not an improved bound for a published or Life-like automaton. |

A useful way to understand proposal 4: the old question was whether an infinite target can be avoided. The quantitative follow-up asks, “If a matching path starts *here*, how many labels can it match before it must stop?” The new formula answers that second question for every start in the specified Thue–Morse graph. The sharp inequality `3L(v) ≤ 8v−1` for positive starts and its equality cases are part of that development.

## Further source-question work

The ten proposals are only one layer of the project. [The open-question notebook](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/research/open-questions/README.md) records four other scoped directions:

- **Genomic identifiability:** exact information and finite-evidence statements, including an obstruction to determining whole-population specieslikeness from certain finite topology-compatible observations. A conditional positive three-taxon law is treated as a known result. This is not DNA-based species classification.
- **Ordinal certificates:** a reachable-state rank characterizes avoidance for labelled graphs; more specific natural ranks and pruning stages are checked for the binary history model. The broader kernel and realizing-population extensions remain outside the proof.
- **Universal avoiding populations:** a language-preserving construction rules out a countable family of locally finite embedding hosts under specified injective edge-preserving or uniformly bounded-stretch maps. Other embedding notions and fixed child caps require different arguments.
- **Maximal specieslike clusters:** for a fixed nonnegative real founding window and explicit birth/branching hypotheses, every organism belongs to a maximal connected, convex, reflecting IAP cluster satisfying that window. [Exact theorem and hypotheses](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/a2644182b147eb40816abb278e00c3c6d4709eb8/notes/REAL-FOUNDER-WINDOW-THEOREM.md). Maximality is relative to those conditions; no unique partition or empirical classifier follows.

## Connections to inheritance and other models

**One random history, two descriptions.** A marker-count transition table does not, by itself, specify a pedigree. The [R1–R6 ancestry construction](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/research/feedback-speciation/PURE-INDUCTION-ANCESTRY.md) uses the same random draws for marker transmission and parent edges. It reproduces every finite count history of its frozen kernel, supplies an explicit conditional bound on unresolved ancestry, and proves the identical-ancestor property almost surely in that model. Equality between a separately defined raw infinite-stream fixation event and the kernel's `fixationValue` remains an open transfer theorem. Exact partial-transmission comparisons are currently rational computations, awaiting Lean instantiation and public admission.

**Wong ancestral recombination graphs.** The project formalized bounded deterministic simplifications of interval-annotated ancestry records and their preservation conditions. A separate probability package constructs the stopped lineage-count law, proves eventual absorption after finitely many jumps almost surely, and attaches exponential physical holding times. [Current Wong status](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/main/RESEARCH-PROGRESS.md#1-wong-count-histories-now-have-elapsed-time) distinguishes this count model from the still-open marked spatial ARG and full source-process correspondence. A 52-family coverage ledger is an inventory, not a completion percentage.

**Time and self-reference.** Exact abstraction theorems identify when a retained observation is sufficient for a chosen future question. A [worked two-model bridge](https://github.com/Sodelin/Work-on-Samuel-Alexander-Research-/blob/022df5fc1240c76c6837e89d38e3009cd020b341/research/open-problems/time-self-reference/controlled-abstraction-bridge/BRIDGE.md) shows a visible bit can fail under a probe action, while one memory bit restores exact prediction for the specified examples. That bridge has written derivations, finite replay, and internal review; its new example/refinement proof is not newly mechanized. The source paper's broad questions, including its source-specific equilibrium framework, remain open.
