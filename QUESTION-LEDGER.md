| Question and origin | Current answer | Remaining boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Which sequences are biologically unavoidable? Samuel A. Alexander's 2013 Section 6 asks the historical classification question. | The separate September 2026 manuscript supplies the classification and the negative construction. This notebook formalizes the binary construction and Alexander's attributed positive theorem, including a binary actual-real-birthdate iff. | The source classification is not our discovery. The general finite-alphabet positive proof is not encoded here, and the classification source is a manuscript, not a refereed publication. |
| Can permanent source genders coexist with arbitrary-target avoidance under a two-child cap? Proposal 1 follows the manuscript's edge-labelled graph and two-copy lift. | **Two is the exact cap threshold.** [CapTwo](notes/CAP-TWO-THRESHOLD.md) gives an eligible directed-line-graph witness for every non-eventually-periodic binary target, exactly two children per vertex, exactly three roots, and exact word-path projection/lifting. Caps below two are impossible. [CapTwoSpecies](notes/CAP-TWO-SPECIES.md) gives an inspecies, specieslike, reflecting whole retained graph simultaneously. | Three-root minimality is not proved. Directed line graphs and edge-to-vertex label recoding are classical; the exact conjunction of population properties is the corollary to assess. The older cap-three witness remains valid but is no longer the best uniform child bound. |
| What does equality in the critical crossing bound force? Proposal 2 extends the original population degree axioms and the checked binary minimum-width theorem. | Full-degree conservation, finite total defects, eventual regularity, and triangular crossing lower bounds are checked. [GeneralRigidity](notes/GENERAL-RIGIDITY-THEOREM.md) proves that eventual width $`k(k+1)/2`$ under child cap $`k`$ forces exactly $`u\to v\iff u<v\le u+k`$ on a tail, for every finite $`k`$. | Equality at one cut alone does not give an entire rigid tail. The result determines adjacency, not universality for arbitrary edge labels. Classification at larger widths remains separate; binary fixed-gender minimum width already implies universality. |
| Can a critical population be represented by finite ports, and what does periodicity imply? Proposal 3 is a structural/computational follow-on. | [PortEncoding](notes/PORT-ENCODING.md) constructs a legal, fair schedule from the actual population, with actual constant cut cardinality and exact labelled-edge decoding for all targets on a tail. Complete eventually periodic schedules realize every infinite word from a fixed finite block of starts, via [PortDynamics](lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/PortDynamics.lean). | The encoder uses classical finite bijections, not a certified canonical sorting algorithm. Bounded width does not imply periodicity. The reverse passage from translation-periodic graph adjacency to a periodic canonical schedule is not claimed. Finite-frontier encoding and finite-state compactness have prior art. |
| How long can Thue-Morse match in $`P_t`$? Proposal 4 sharpens a notebook follow-on; the source manuscript Section 5 already asks for useful quantitative fixed-target bounds. | [FullHeight](notes/FULL-HEIGHT-PROOF.md) proves the complete formula for actual attained maxima, including $`L(0)=1`$. [DigitRecurrence](notes/DIGIT-RECURRENCE.md) certifies an executable ten-coordinate integer binary recursion. Sharp $`3L(v)\le8v-1`$ for $`v\ge1`$, exact dyadic equality, baseline first-hit results, and real coefficient optimality are checked. | The optional literal integer-module 2-regularity wrapper has passed statement review but still awaits compilation. No minimality of ten coordinates or bit-complexity theorem is claimed. Equivalent earlier formulas and literature priority need external review. |
| What survives phase changes? Proposal 5 rebuilds the graph as well as shifting its target. | For $`t_a(k)=t(k+a)`$, $`3L_a(v)\le8v+5a-1`$ for positive starts, with optimal coefficient $`8/3`$. [PhaseExtremal](notes/PHASE-EXTREMAL-FORMALIZATION.md) proves equality iff $`a\le q=2^n`$, $`v=3q-a-1`$, and $`L_a(v)=8q-a-3`$. [PhaseHeight](notes/PHASE-HEIGHT-FORMALIZATION.md) computes exact maxima at every phase and start, including zero. | The finite frontier algorithm does not supply a joint phase/index digit recurrence, a closed form for all phases, or a runtime complexity bound. |
| What quantitative information yields a useful avoidance rate? Proposal 6 separates qualitative aperiodicity from effective bounds. | [QuantitativeModulus](notes/QUANTITATIVE-MODULUS-THEOREM.md) derives period/antiperiod break moduli and proves $`\ell<B_{v+1}`$ with $`B_0=0`$, $`B_{r+1}=M(v,B_r)`$; windows of length at most $`R`$ give $`\ell<(v+1)R`$. Conversely, [FiniteAvoidance](notes/FINITE-AVOIDANCE-FORMALIZATION.md) gives aperiodic targets with actual finite maxima exceeding any supplied growth function along strictly increasing starts; every start has a finite maximum. | Aperiodicity alone gives no word-independent upper rate. Modulus existence uses classical choice; the clock is executable when a modulus is supplied. A separate computability theorem for searching target bits is not formalized. |
| What survives finite target edits, and what is the best universal allowance? Proposal 7 extends the earlier checked transport theorem. | If $`s=t`$ from $`m`$ onward, [FiniteEditExact](notes/FINITE-EDIT-EXACT-FORMALIZATION.md) gives exact maxima by a finite frontier/shifted-suffix decomposition, including early extinction and zero. The bound $`3L_s(v)\le8v+8m-1`$ for $`v\ge1`$ has the optimal universal integer constant $`8m-1`$. Equality is characterized completely by dyadic $`q\ge m`$, $`v=3q-m-1`$, $`L_s(v)=8q-3`$, and a finite all-two-step prefix test. The optimal coefficient remains $`8/3`$. | A fixed edited target can have a smaller best additive constant. A finite global classification of that individual optimum and its maximizing starts remains separate from the proved universal constant and its complete equality test. |
| What survives productive pruning, including at maximal clusters? Proposal 8 uses Alexander's inspecies and IAP framework. | [ProductiveCore](notes/PRODUCTIVE-CORE-THEOREM.md) constructs the actual retained/reindexed binary population, preserves child caps and the entire infinite word language, and yields an inspecies under whole-graph IAP. Unrestricted maximal specieslike clusters in natural birth order are descendant-closed and pruning commutes. Convex reflection clusters also satisfy commutation by a different proof. | Common-ancestor/reflection-constrained maximality does not force descendant closure; a checked source-example counterexample separates the notions. Arbitrary nonmaximal subsets can fail commutation, and finite paths need not survive pruning. Labelled eligibility is binary and naturally indexed; this is not an empirical species criterion. |
| Can a common-ancestor cluster inherit population eligibility after finite repair? Proposal 9 comes from the exact source cones. | [BoundaryRepair](notes/BOUNDARY-REPAIR-THEOREM.md) proves that a fixed infinite retained set admits an eligible deletion-only repair iff its incoming-label deficiency set is finite. The canonical repair preserves the greatest possible edge relation, has the smallest possible root set, preserves caps and avoidance, and changes only finitely many retained edges. Both source-cone repairs are computed exactly. | The retained set is fixed and only edge deletion is allowed; adding/relabeling edges or further vertex deletion is a different optimization. Repair need not preserve connectedness: the repaired $`C_0`$ has an isolated zero. It therefore need not preserve all specieslike/common-ancestor properties. |
| Can a stateful local certificate improve a static CA speed bound? Proposal 10 is motivated by Alexander's lifeline application. | [StatefulCA](notes/STATEFUL-CA-FORMALIZATION.md) proves a complete synthetic three-state rule-to-evolution example. Every valid fixed two-label static certificate has east support exactly one; a state potential forces zero horizontal translation for every finite nonempty spaceship, and a full finite oscillator is checked. [StatefulCAReal](notes/STATEFUL-CA-REAL.md) proves the attained static optimum over literal real convex hulls. | This is a strict comparison of specified certificate classes for one synthetic rule. It is not a new bound for a published or binary Life-like rule, and the support point at one is not an actual spaceship. Potentials, cycle means, and existing published-rule bounds retain their prior attribution. |
| How do IAP, inspecies, and common-ancestor cones interact? A cross-paper question using Alexander's 2013 and 2026 definitions. | General finite/cofinite IAP and inspecies criteria, a root-cone classification criterion, and the exact two-cone specialization remain checked. The productive-core and repair results above sharpen when population eligibility and cluster predicates coexist. | Alexander's cofinite-descendant theorem and earlier root-cone examples are precedents, not new general mechanisms. Broad common-ancestor-constrained existence and empirical species delimitation are not established by these specializations. |

The original finite sharp-height question and its full-height extension were
formulated in this notebook. They should not be described as thirteen-year-old
finite-length conjectures. Alexander's historical classification question is
separate: his 2013 work supplies the population model and positive direction;
the September 2026 manuscript supplies the target-dependent negative
construction and classification. No individual authorship beyond the verified
source records is inferred for that separate manuscript.

Method attribution is also explicit. The line-digraph recoding has classical
antecedents in Harary and Norman and Orlin; Oliveira supplies relevant
finite-frontier slice encodings; Young, Tarjan, and Orlin supply graph-potential
methods, with Karp and Dasdan and Gupta supplying cycle-mean antecedents.

[port/CA comparison](notes/source-audits/PROPOSALS3-10-SOURCE-AUDIT.md) distinguish
