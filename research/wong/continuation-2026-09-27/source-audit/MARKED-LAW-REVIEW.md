The architecture is sound for a continuous-breakpoint Big ARG. For each
event index t and finite frontier s, it supplies an independent mark M(t,s).
The law of that mark is exactly a product of uniform lineage, uniform
unordered pair, and uniform interior breakpoint. The whole innovation array
is independent of the whole checked count-and-clock path. Because
N × Finset(N) is countable, the intersection of all the coordinatewise
probability-one validity events remains probability one. An adaptively
chosen frontier therefore has a valid mark on that same event.

Validity and the conditional distribution are different obligations. The
current `innovation_marginal`, `innovations_independent`, and
`timed_count_marginal` are appropriate ingredients. They do not on their own
prove that the mark actually consumed by a recursively computed recorder has
the required conditional law. No conceptual obstruction was found; the
following explicit lemma closes the gap.

## Adaptive selection lemma

Let Z denote the complete count-and-clock coordinate. For a fixed t, let
F*_t be the sigma-field generated by Z and all coordinates M(r,s) with r<t.
Suppose S_t is a Finset(N)-valued, F*_t-measurable random variable, and define
the selected mark U_t=M(t,S_t). Then, for every event A in F*_t and every
Borel mark set B,

    P(A ∩ {U_t ∈ B}) = ∫_A markLaw(S_t)(B) dP.

This is a conditional-kernel statement. It proves more than support and
avoids treating a varying frontier as a fixed coordinate.

Proof route:

1. Prove the entire row `(s ↦ M(t,s))` is independent of Z and all earlier
   rows. Product independence gives the Z part; independence of disjoint
   sets of coordinate indices gives the earlier-row part.
2. Each fixed coordinate M(t,s) has law `markLaw(s)` and is independent of
   F*_t. Hence for A_s=A∩{S_t=s},
   `P(A_s ∩ {M(t,s)∈B}) = P(A_s)·markLaw(s)(B)`.
3. Finset(N) is countable. Partition A into the disjoint measurable A_s and
   sum the identity. The sum on the right is the integral in the statement.
4. Measurability of U_t follows from the same countable partition, or a
   measurable evaluation map `(M,s) ↦ M(t,s)` for a countable index type.

For a Lean-friendly statement without conditional expectation, use the
displayed measure/integral identity or an equivalent nonnegative-test
function identity. A general test-function version is

    ∫ f(Z, M_before_t) g(U_t) dP
      = ∫ f(Z, M_before_t) (∫ g(u) dmarkLaw(S_t)(u)) dP,

for nonnegative measurable f and g. Indicator specializations recover the
uniform 1/|S_t| lineage mass, uniform 1/choose(|S_t|,2) pair mass, and the
normalized Lebesgue cut law. The frontier must depend only on earlier rows;
dependence on any mark in row t, even an ostensibly unused alternative
frontier coordinate, can bias adaptive selection.

Since F*_t contains the full count path, it already knows whether the next
event is a split or a merger. Thus the same statement remains true after
restricting A to either count-jump event. This is useful for showing that
event-type selection does not bias the chosen spatial mark.

## The filtration for the full event law is smaller

F*_t is appropriate for the mark lemma but is too large for a next-wait
exponential assertion: it contains the complete clock path, including the
next clock. For the full source transition rule, use a natural past
sigma-field generated by K_0,…,K_t, E_0,…,E_(t−1), and the consumed spatial
marks from events strictly before t. An enlarged version may include all
earlier innovation rows, which simplifies proofs.

Under that smaller filtration, prove or derive these three factors:

- The next count has the checked `jumpKernel` law at K_t. Its Markov
  prefix/next-state identity must be lifted through the independent clock
  and spatial product coordinates. A one-time count marginal is insufficient.
- E_t is an independent unit exponential; scaling by q(K_t) gives the next
  waiting law. Only a>0, b≥0, and pre-hit K_t≥2 are used for positivity.
- The adaptive spatial selection has `markLaw(S_t)` and is conditionally
  independent of the next count choice and wait. The adaptive lemma above
  allows conditioning on the whole count-and-clock coordinate for this part.

Their product is the standard marked jump kernel. It may be stated directly
as a finite-prefix cylinder law; an extra right-continuous real-time state
construction is not required merely to establish this discrete event-record
law and its finite physical stopping time.

## Recorder and graph measurability

The recorder fold must be measurable and causal: S_0 is deterministic; S_t,
IDs allocated so far, and accumulated records use only counts up through t
and spatial marks at indices below t. The update at t reads M(t,S_t) once.
Fresh IDs and the update may be deterministic functions of the old finite
state and the selected mark. No choice in the update may inspect future
rows to obtain a convenient valid realization.

An economical proof is induction on each finite prefix, first for a raw
encoding whose data are natural IDs, finite ID collections, finite record
lists, real dates, and real cuts. Counts and IDs have discrete measurable
spaces; reals have their Borel measurable spaces. Finite conditionals and
finite-list recursion then preserve measurability. Express graph validity
as an invariant/property of that encoding, and attach its proof afterwards.
For the random stopped output, partition by the measurable first-hit index,
which is natural-valued, and use the fixed-prefix measurability result.

Avoid making every subset of an uncountable real-cut graph state measurable
merely to simplify typeclass inference: that is a stronger sigma-field than
the one produced by Borel sampling, and the recording map need not be
measurable into it. A countable disjoint union of finite raw-record spaces
with their ordinary Borel structures is a faithful option. A stream of
option-tagged event records with the product sigma-field is another.

Prove that active-cardinality and event-time extraction from the actual
record agree almost surely with the old count/wait observation. Their
pushforward law then follows from almost-everywhere equality and the proved
count/clock marginal. It must be the recorder's counts and dates in this
statement, not the unchanged input projection. Almost-sure stopping and
nonexplosion transfer through this equality together with the count-to-number
of-recorded-events invariant.

The old endpoint-only decoder may be used only after a verified
identity-preserving encoding. Immediate split/reunion must remain a legal
recorded history. The construction currently names continuous cuts explicitly,
which correctly preserves the discrete/continuous source qualification in
[APPENDIX-B-CONTRACT.md](APPENDIX-B-CONTRACT.md).

## Verdict and shortest next route

The draft defines appropriate component measures and probability-one support.
No mathematical correction to its choice of product space is required.
The remaining mathematical acceptance checks are: causal measurable fold;
adaptive conditional mark-kernel identity; correct full transition factorization;
identity-preserving event/interval encoding including reunion; and the actual
recorder's count/time projection theorem. The shortest route is a reusable
adaptive-selection lemma plus induction on finite recorder prefixes, followed
by countable stopping-index gluing. A product marginal alone must not be
reported as completion of B04/B05.

The shortest sigma-field proof stays on the innovation-array space first.
For an index ix let `m_ix = MeasurableSpace.comap (fun M => M ix) inferInstance`.
Define `pastSigma t = ⨆ ix, ⨆ (_ : ix.1 < t), m_ix`. Each m_ix lies below the
ambient product sigma-field by `(measurable_pi_apply ix).comap_le`.
Apply `indep_iSup_of_disjoint` to the index sets `{(t,s)}` and
`{ix | ix.1 < t}`, using `innovations_independent` converted with
`iIndepFun_iff_iIndep`. Simplify the singleton supremum. This proves
`Indep (m_(t,s)) (pastSigma t) innovationLaw` for every s.

Prove the adaptive identity for arbitrary S measurable from `pastSigma t`
and arbitrary A measurable in that same sigma-field. The fibre
`A ∩ {M | S M = s}` is measurable in the past. The fixed present-coordinate
event `{M | M(t,s) ∈ B}` is measurable in m_(t,s). `Indep_iff` gives the
factorization without constructing a conditional expectation. Its second
factor becomes `markLaw(s)(B)` using `innovation_marginal` and `Measure.map_apply`.
Countable disjoint additivity and `lintegral_iUnion` finish the formula.

For the Z-dependent recorder, define S on `Z × Innovations`, measurable for
the product of Z's usual sigma-field and `pastSigma t`. Fix z and apply the
preceding lemma to S(z,·) and to the section of A. Integrate the resulting
identity in z with `lintegral_prod`. This avoids proving a separate theorem
that independence from each of two components implies independence from
their joint pair; pairwise independence would be insufficient for that
shortcut. The actual product structure is what justifies this lift.
