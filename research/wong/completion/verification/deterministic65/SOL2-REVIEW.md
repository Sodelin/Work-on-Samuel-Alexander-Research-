## Adversarial resolution

**MRCA.** The minimal DAG with edges `p→s₁`, `p→s₂`, `q→s₁`, `q→s₂`, samples `{s₁,s₂}`, and incomparable `p,q` has two common ancestors but no latest one. It defeats unconditional MRCA existence; it does **not** satisfy `G.UniqueParentAt x`. `exists_unique_mrca` lists all three needed premises (`samples.Nonempty`, unique local parents, and an existing common ancestor). `IsMRCA` is defined as a common ancestor below *every* common ancestor, so `mrca_unique` needs no further unique-parent premise once two such witnesses are supplied. `distinct_sample_roots_no_common`, `empty_samples_no_edges`, and `singleton_sample_is_mrca` cover separate boundary cases. `TruncatedAt` is `ExtractedAt ∧ ¬ Common(child)`; `supported_but_removed` and `no_incoming_mrca` defeat the false equation with ordinary sample support. `truncation_iff_below_mrca` requires local parent uniqueness. `sample_paths_preserved` and `mrca_sample_paths_preserved` are correctly limited to named endpoints; `finite_mrca_truncation` retains samples, preserves those paths, inherits unique parenthood when present, and adds no breakpoints.

**Intervals.** The attempted `Nat` gap example does not refute the statements: the merge joins exactly equal boundary endpoints, while the gap `[1,2)` is observable at coordinate `1` even in a discrete order. `mergeAdjacent_covers`, `mergeAdjacent_separated (hL : Ordered L)`, and `separated_semantic_unique (hL : Separated L) (hM : Separated M)` have the needed premises. `Ordered` means every prior `hi ≤` later `lo`; `Separated` strengthens this to `<`. `join_contains` and `mergeAdjacent_lo`/`mergeAdjacent_hi` support membership preservation and reuse of input endpoints; they do not license merging overlap or unsorted input. For actual graph records, `canonicalize_at` uses the full `AtLocus` relation; `canonicalize_nonempty`, `canonicalize_canonical`, `canonicalize_maximal`, `canonicalize_breakpoints`, and `canonicalize_idempotent` support canonical storage, omission of empty annotations, no new breakpoints, and storage idempotence. The record construction is classical/noncomputable; only `mergeAdjacent` is an executable list algorithm.

**Normal form.** The unary sampled-ancestor and isolated-catalogue-node witnesses are excluded by the exact `NoNonsampleUnary` definition: it demands two distinct outgoing children only for an *incident nonsample* node. `retained_nonsample_branches` uses local `UniqueParentAt` to prevent two supported branches from rejoining, and `represented_contraction_normal` transfers this to a graph whose edges satisfy the displayed `Contract` equivalence. The aggregate `canonical_resolved_normal_form` yields an actual finite `GARG` with the samples preserved and edges equal to contraction of **truncated sample ancestry**, and it supplies sample paths, conditional MRCA paths, maximal per-pair intervals, and `canonicalize H = H`. Its no-unary and unique-parent conclusions are conditional on `G.UniqueParentAt x`. It leaves unused IDs in the node catalogue and does not assert whole-pipeline idempotence.

**Sample-array false control.** A finite acyclic graph with a sampled isolated node `s` and an unrelated edge `p→c` has a valid local edge invisible to tracing ancestors of `s`. Thus the paper's Appendix E statement that one can reconstruct *all original edges* from sample-traced local parent arrays under “no requirement on the structure ... beyond the basic definitions” is too broad as written, unless all original local edges are sample-supported. The selected theorems avoid this error: `ExtractedAt` is the sample-supported relation used by truncation and simplification, while the separate `canonicalize_at` theorem reconstructs full `AtLocus` only because it takes full `AtLocus` as input. These are different observations, and neither theorem proves an unconditional sample-array inverse.
