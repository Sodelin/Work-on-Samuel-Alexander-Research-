## Selected mathematical question

For a finite collection of reproductive demes with a fully specified infinite parenthood graph, characterize exactly when Alexander's finite/cofinite identical-ancestor-point (IAP) axiom holds from the pattern of recurring cross-deme parenthood.

This is a **newly posed, source-motivated application question**. Alexander supplies the target axiom. Earlier structured-genealogy and temporal-network results supply essential mechanisms. We have not found or claimed an author-designated conjecture with this exact statement.

The candidate answer is a classification by strongly connected components of recurring reproductive links, under explicit local dissemination and persistent-occupancy assumptions. All but finitely many members of an infinite IAP set must belong to one such component, and this condition is sufficient. The assumptions exclude local lineage extinction. The theorem does not identify maximal species, infer an infinite future from finite observations, or prove that human cultural groups are biological species.

## Three bounded proof deliverables

1. **Pedigree bridge.** Derive eventual ancestry from actual parenthood, then prove the IAP/component equivalence. Do not assume the derived reachability condition as an axiom.
2. **Model boundary.** Prove that persistent cultural differences can coexist with continuing neutral gene exchange in an explicit two-deme majority-learning model. This tests a tempting implication; it is not a complete genetic/epigenetic/cultural feedback model.
3. **Published-model refinement.** For Fogarty–Zhang–Feldman's actual affinity-bias recursions, prove absence of simultaneous genetic and cultural polymorphism at a fixed point for positive selection over the full probability range of affinity parameters. Classify this as a proof clarification/parameter extension until prior-art review establishes anything stronger. The completed lane also derives a uniform fixation rate under arbitrary generation-dependent affinity probabilities, from a standard positive-matrix comparison.
