## Precisely scoped result and source relationship

Planidin et al. (2025), [Conclusion §5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12440623/), proposes finite-population models as a way to investigate changes in the relative strength of genetic and epigenetic barriers. That is a research direction, not the exact numerical conjecture below.

The question addressed here is: with m=1/4, s=r=1/2, matched secondary-contact initialization, and final offspring sampling, can the ordering between a mutation-free selected genetic locus and a completely environmentally reset epigenetic locus differ from the deterministic ordering?

The finite limiting probabilities, deterministic ordering from generation four onward, and their joint ranking reversal are checked in Lean. The [combined receipt](verification/combined-receipt.json) covers 34 selected endpoints across four modules. Deterministic common-limit existence and derivations from the full phased life cycle remain written and independently audited, outside those modules.

Neither the pure-induction scalar formula nor neutral-marker fixation as a measure is new. The potentially useful addition is the explicit matched finite comparison, an exact certificate, and the ranking reversal. Prior-work screening did not establish priority. Do not advertise this as a first-ever finite epigenetic model or a major solved conjecture.

## Open mathematical questions generated by the comparison

These are new precise follow-up questions formulated in this project, motivated by the source direction. They are not presented as questions quoted verbatim from the source, longstanding named conjectures, or already solved results.

1. **A finite population size that restores the genetic advantage.** Extend the stated final-offspring-sampling model to N diploid adults per deme, retaining all other parameters and the same pulse. Let R_gen(N) and R_epi(N) be the corresponding fixation-based measures. Does some finite N satisfy R_gen(N)>R_epi(N)? If yes, find the least one, or certified lower and upper bounds. The deterministic result does not itself prove that a finite such N exists.

2. **The large-population and long-time limits.** For the same family, does lim_{N→∞}R_gen(N) equal the deterministic marker barrier? Quantify any error and state the order of limits. At any finite N the selected genetic variation can eventually be lost; this is different from first taking a deterministic population limit. A proof needs to control these competing timescales.

3. **Robustness of the strict reversal.** Find an explicit parameter neighborhood around (m,s,r)=(1/4,1/2,1/2), and a range of imperfect induction rates, for which the matched reversal remains true. The current count reduction discards linkage phase only at r=1/2. Other r require phased states or a proved alternative reduction.

4. **Dependence on the finite life cycle.** Determine which reversal conclusions persist when migration first samples an integer set of adults, mating excludes selfing, or population size can fluctuate. Such changes define new kernels. Their results cannot be inferred from the present fixed-size final-sampling theorem.

5. **An explicit ancestry bridge.** Add parent identities to the individual-based finite model, then compare neutral-marker fixation with Alexander's generator and identical-ancestor predicates under a stated infinite extension. Determine conditions under which one implies the other, and construct counterexamples when it does not. A biallelic neutral marker and an organism-level parent graph are different mathematical observations.
