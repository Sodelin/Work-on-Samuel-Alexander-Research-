## Main remaining mathematical target: extinction-aware pedigree classification

The current bridge assumes that every organism spreads throughout its deme. A biologically less restrictive theorem would allow a lineage either to disappear locally or to establish and spread. Raw cross-deme edge recurrence is insufficient because those crossings can repeatedly involve irrelevant clans.

A precise research task is to define successful transmission of an established ancestral lineage, state measurable survival/mixing conditions, and determine whether a suitable lineage-dependent recurrent graph gives a finite/cofinite IAP classification. The challenge is to derive the interface from reproduction instead of assuming eventual common ancestry. This is newly posed here; no claim that every version is unresolved in the literature.

## Source-proposed finite-population question

For the life cycle in [Planidin et al.](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2025.1217), construct a finite two-deme version preserving the order of migration, selection, recombination/mating and epimutation. Define the neutral migrant-marker outcome, the matched genetic comparator and the initial state.

Then prove a parameter-conditional comparison of expected marker transmission, or give a certified counterexample to a proposed universal comparison. Fixation probabilities and finite-horizon transmission are different targets. The authors' suggestion is conditional, so a counterexample to universal improvement would not refute their published statement.

## Source-proposed nonvertical-learning question

For [Fogarty, Zhang and Feldman](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tpb.2025.08.003), specify a cultural-learning kernel that includes nonparents. State whether learners select models by genetic type, phenotype, deme or global frequency, and whether learning occurs before selection.

The task is to characterize equilibria and association under that exact kernel, with the vertical model recovered at a declared boundary parameter. Choosing a kernel and proving a convenient theorem must not be represented as solving a previously precise conjecture which the authors never stated.

## Why these questions matter to the user's idea

The project preserves the proposed feedback chain: inherited biological state, regulation and learned behaviour can influence reproduction; reproduction determines the pedigree. The current theorem addresses the last step under explicit assumptions. The earlier causal steps remain modelling and empirical obligations. The result does not treat societies, cultures or human population labels as biological species.
