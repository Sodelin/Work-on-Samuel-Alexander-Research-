# Genome ownership and sampling: publication audit

Audit date: 29 September 2026. This is a bounded primary-source and statement audit of the two existing pending submissions. It introduces no new proof, changes no proof source, and makes no external submission. The accompanying [field drafts](VIBEMATH-DRAFTS.json) are for the publication coordinator to review. The [evidence ledger](EVIDENCE.json) records sources, exact queries, access limits and claim-level findings.

**Verdict: present the work as a coordinated information-loss and recovery result.** The ownership theorem constructs two tightly constrained pedigrees with the same complete realized copy history and opposite whole-population identical-ancestor properties. The sampling theorem then characterizes exactly which ownership observations recover that property in the construction's delayed-split family. These are complete answers to two precise project-posed questions. The present search does not establish closure of an externally posed open conjecture, worldwide priority, or catalog eligibility.

The best contribution-first description is: **retaining every realized transmission still leaves a consequential gap when diploid ownership is omitted; in one checked family, the timing of additional ownership observations exactly determines whether that gap can be repaired.** The force comes from preserving the stated constraints together. It does not come from discovering for the first time that genetic and genealogical ancestry differ.

## What is proved and where

The fixed genomic history consists of eight lanes, with one edge from each copy to the same lane in the next natural-number generation. Every displayed interval is `[0,1)`. At each generation, one of three pairings groups the eight copies into four organisms:

| Pairing | Four organism blocks |
|---|---|
| M0 | (01), (23), (45), (67) |
| M1 | (12), (34), (56), (70) |
| M2 | (12), (30), (56), (74) |

The mixing schedule alternates M0 and M1. A splitting schedule agrees through an even time S >= 2, then alternates M0 and M2. Parent-child edges are exactly the owner-images of actual copy transmissions. The observation retains the copy graph at every time, plus ownership through any prescribed finite generation N. It withholds subsequent pairings. The copy graph models one unrecombined abstract region; nucleotide sequences and a recombination probability law are absent.

The single-query theorem itself does not require B >= 2: below 2 its bounded family contains only mixing. Use the substantive B >= 2 form in the submission. |

For clarity, IAP here is the exact predicate in [SpeciesBridge](../../../lean/SamuelAlexanderResearch/SpeciesBridge.lean), line 177: every member has finitely many descendants or finitely many non-descendants within the set. Whole-population maximal specieslike status follows in the mixing example because the whole graph is connected, convex and IAP. Maximality of the whole graph is immediate once those conditions hold; it should not be presented as a separate difficult classification theorem.

The observation query is the co-ownership of lanes 0 and 3. Its exact value is always false under mixing and true precisely at odd times after S under splitting. Thus an unbounded supply of sampled odd times detects every allowed split. A bounded supply leaves room for a later one. The infinite-record decoder is the proposition that no sampled observation is positive. This is a mathematical classification of complete records, with no claim of a uniformly terminating procedure on the all-negative record. A positive observation certifies the non-IAP branch within the family.

These comparisons support the **candidate increment** as the simultaneous constraint set and the opposite-IAP consequence, followed by the exact sampling boundary for that construction. They do not establish global priority. The sampling argument is short once its query law and the base IAP separation are known. The nontrivial construction work is arranging every local reproduction control, arbitrary finite agreement, weak connectivity and an all-time ancestry contrast in one witness, and checking the source-model maps. That is the difficulty worth explaining.

## Which open question can be cited?

The source record should distinguish three roles:

1. **Exact question answered:** the project-posed ownership inverse problem, and its project-posed static-mask recovery continuation. The checked results resolve these exact questions.
2. **External mathematical setting:** Alexander's IAP/specieslike definitions, Wong's genomic-ancestry representation, and the established pedigree reconstruction literature. These are relevant source connections, not evidence that those authors posed our exact question.
3. **Broader biology agenda:** the repository relates information lost by projection and observation adequacy to Abramsky et al., [“Open questions about time and self-reference in living systems”](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.261059). The existing [source ledger](../../open-problems/time-self-reference/problem-ledger.json) identifies Q04 with §4.2, p.11, and explicitly leaves the full author question open. This audit could not freshly retrieve that DOI. Preserve the connection as motivation, with its existing correspondence record, rather than elevating it into a newly verified source-question solution.

Thatte's §6 provides an actual historical open-problem locator about stochastic identifiability and tractable invariants, but changing its observation map would change the problem. Steel–Hein's fixed-size-subpedigree question was already answered by Thatte. These are useful comparisons to cite, not open-problem labels to borrow. No current unresolved external question precisely equivalent to either submitted theorem was established in this bounded audit.
