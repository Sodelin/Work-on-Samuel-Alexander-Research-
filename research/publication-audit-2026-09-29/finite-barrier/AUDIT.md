## The result a reader should see first

Start both models with locally adapted selected alleles, `EEbb` in deme 1 and `eebb` in deme 2. Use migration `m = 1/4`, additive selection `s = 1/2`, and recombination `r = 1/2`. Mark the first deme-1-to-deme-2 migrant contribution once, before destination selection; ordinary migration then continues. Compare mutation-free inheritance with complete environmental resetting of the selected state after mating. In the finite model, sample one diploid offspring independently in each deme. Gamete sampling permits selfing; migration uses weighted reproductive pools rather than a random integer migrant census.

The exact comparison is:

| Quantity | Genetic inheritance | Pure environmental induction |
|---|---:|---:|
| Finite limiting global marker-fixation probability | `323808055/4466354606` | `1/14` |
| Finite fixation barrier, `RI = 1 - p/(1/8)` | `937945083/2233177303` | `3/7` |
| Deterministic mean-marker barrier, every biological generation at least four | at least `1027087570805/2255346713174`, strictly greater than `3/7` | `3/7` |

The finite epigenetic advantage is exactly `19130904/2233177303`, approximately `0.00857` in RI units. The sign is positive in the finite comparison and negative in every deterministic comparison from generation four onward. The theorem concerns this explicit model and experiment; it is not a population-size monotonicity theorem or a measurement of species formation in nature.

The mathematical work is more than obtaining two numerical outputs. It connects a rational harmonic certificate for an 81-state kernel to actual finite-time marginal probabilities, proves boundary closure and a uniform terminal-mass bound, and obtains an error at most `(99/100)^n`. On the deterministic side, an exact four-generation calculation enters an invariant convex region that certifies every later comparison. The nonlinearity of selection normalization explains why adding conditionally unbiased sampling need not preserve the deterministic trajectory or its ordering.
