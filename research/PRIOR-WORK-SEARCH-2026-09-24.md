## Coverage and screening rule

We looked for (a) an exact earlier classification under Alexander's finite-root, finite-outdegree, full-incoming-label population axioms; (b) a prior theorem implying the `P_s` ancestry and cluster statements; (c) mathematical biology cluster models with closely matching predicates; and (d) adjacent word, automatic-sequence, and graph-end results that might look equivalent but have different quantifiers. A source was considered **subsuming** only after checking its theorem statement and assumptions against the claim; title or abstract similarity was insufficient. Preprints, published papers, the public classification manuscript, and this AI-assisted notebook are kept distinct. Search hits repeating the classification manuscript, such as [VibeMathed's problem page](https://vibemathed.com/problem/classification-of-biologically-unavoidable-sequences), were not counted as independent prior proofs.

The ordinary `web.run` searches below returned ranked results with an unspecified underlying retrieval cap. Exact negative counts therefore mean only “no verified match in inspected results.” The alphaXiv discovery tool returned ranked arXiv candidates, not an exhaustive arXiv query. PubMed is an independent biomedical publication index, not a completeness check for combinatorics. No search covered all languages, theses, MathSciNet/zbMATH paywalled records, or every forward citation.

## Adversarial comparison added 25 September UTC

An independent pass reread [Alexander 2013, Proposition 6](https://arxiv.org/html/1201.2869) and [Alexander 2026, Example 14(1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.05274v1) as possible **subsuming** results, then compared their hypotheses with the exact `P_s` graph. It found a useful strict-layering distinction: in Example 14(1) at `k=2`, all edges join consecutive two-vertex generations, and any valid binary incoming-edge labelling realizes every binary infinite word by backward choice and König's lemma. This deduction and its broader strictly layered version are proved in the [generalization note](../notes/SPECIESLIKE-GENERALIZATION.md). The pass also checked the scope of [Dress et al. 2010, Theorem 2](https://www.math.canterbury.ac.nz/~m.steel/Non_UC/files/research/species_jtb.pdf): its finite-DAG hypothesis does not imply an infinite IAP result.
